# BEHIND THE HUG — COMPLETE CHANNEL STRATEGY
*Written as if I were the human running this channel. Every claim grounded in the channel's real data.*

## 1. THE AUDIENCE (everything flows from this)
- **65+ years old: 29%. Female: 58%. US: 28%. TV: 30.5% of watch time. 95% non-subscribers.**
- Translation: I am making slow, warm, LARGE-text television for American grandmothers — not "content" for scrollers.
- Every decision filter: *"Would a 70-year-old woman in Ohio watch this on her TV at 8 PM and cry happy tears?"* If no, it doesn't get made.

## 2. POSITIONING (one sentence)
**"True-feeling stories about dogs and the people who'd do anything for them."**
- Not "pet videos." Not "soldier compilations." A *story channel* where the dog is always the emotional protagonist.
- The moat: nobody in this niche runs a used-list anti-repetition system. We do now. 50 stories in, ours still feel fresh — theirs feel samey.

## 3. CONTENT PILLARS (never leave these)
1. **Homecomings** (hero pillar) — soldier/dad/grandpa returns. Highest emotion, proven.
2. **Old souls** — senior dogs + senior humans. Direct mirror of the audience. Lowest competition.
3. **Second chances** — rescues, shelters, strays. Evergreen shareability.
4. **Working dogs** — K9, therapy, service. Built-in credibility + veteran community shares.
- Rule: 60% of output from pillars 1–2 (proven), 40% from 3–4 (growth).

## 4. FORMAT STACK (three formats, one brand)
- **Long-form 8–12 min** (the money): TV audience watches long. 8+ min = mid-roll ads = 3–5× RPM of shorts. 2 per week.
- **Avatar long-form** (the trust): same stories, host on camera for intro/bridges/outro. Face builds parasocial loyalty → subscribers (we're at 95% non-subscribed; this fixes it). 1 per week, alternating with #1.
- **Shorts 15–60s** (the funnel): cut-downs + the 1000-prompt bank. 3–4 per week. Their ONLY job: feed the long-form. Every short ends with "full story on the channel."

## 5. THE RETENTION SKELETON (every long-form, no exceptions)
- **0:00–0:18** — Hook: the strange behavior + the unanswered question. No intro music, no "welcome back" before the hook.
- **0:18–5:00** — Backstory + bond (warm beats interleaved; never 3 sad beats in a row).
- **~55%** — Setback: hope appears, then breaks. (This is where competitors lose people; we *plan* it.)
- **~70%** — Twist: the misdirection flips. The rewatchable moment.
- **Last 60s** — 2-beat ending: setup line + quotable landing line. This is what gets commented and shared.
- **Pacing:** ~120 wpm narration, [pause] cues, large burned-in captions. Senior ears and eyes.

## 6. TITLE + THUMBNAIL SYSTEM
- **Titles:** number + time + emotional payoff. ("He Waited 400 Days…", "12-Year-Old Dog Walked 2 Miles…"). ≤ 70 chars. Title's promise = the longest beat in the video. Never outrun the story.
- **Thumbnails:** dog's face, extreme close-up, eyes visible, 3 words max ("HE WAITED", "SHE KNEW"). Warm color grade. Test 2 variants per video for the first 10.
- **Descriptions:** first 2 lines = emotional summary + subscribe. Tags: 10, niche-specific.

## 7. PRODUCTION PIPELINE (the engine — already built)
`behind-the-hug-engine/`: batch plan (avoidance rules) → 5 ideas per batch (full ESR template) → register → per story: **Title → Voiceover → Outline → Image + Image-to-Video prompts** → produce → publish → used-list updates itself.
- Throughput target: 1 batch (5 ideas) per week → 2–3 produced videos/week. The engine is the employee that never sleeps.

## 8. 90-DAY PLAN
- **Days 1–30 (FIND):** 8 long-form (2 per pillar) + 24 shorts. No scaling. Watch: which pillar gets the best *average view duration* (not views — duration). Kill the weakest pillar's frequency.
- **Days 31–60 (DOUBLE DOWN):** 70% of output to the winning pillar. Start avatar format here (audience now knows the brand). First community post: "which story made you cry?"
- **Days 61–90 (SERIES):** Turn winners into playlists/series ("The Waiting Ones", "Old Souls"). Series = binge = session time = algorithm love. Pitch first sponsor (senior-dog products).

## 9. MONETIZATION (in order)
1. **AdSense long-form** — the core. 8+ min, mid-rolls.
2. **Affiliate** — senior-dog niche products (joint supplements, ramps, orthopedic beds). A 70-year-old with a 12-year-old Lab BUGHT intent. One link in description + pinned comment.
3. **Digital product** — "The Senior Dog Care Manual" style guide (already in the book pipeline — cross-promote).
4. **Sponsors** — pet brands pay for 65+ female US attention. Media kit at 50k subs.

## 10. WHAT I'D NEVER DO
- Never fake "real footage" — AI label stays on, always. One exposed fake kills a trust channel forever.
- Never grief-bait with dog death as the twist. Sad is fine; cruel is not. The audience came to feel *hope*.
- Never chase trends outside the 4 pillars. A viral cat video that brings the wrong audience poisons the algorithm profile.
- Never publish without the checklist: character lock consistent? captions large? pauses kept? title promise = longest beat?
- Never let the used-list go stale. The day we repeat ourselves is the day we become the competitor.

## 11. THE ONE BET
If I had to bet the channel on one thing: **"Old Souls" (senior dog + senior human).** It's the least competitive, it mirrors the actual audience (29% are 65+), and nobody — nobody — is doing it as a *system*. That's the blue ocean. Homecomings get us discovered; Old Souls make us irreplaceable.
