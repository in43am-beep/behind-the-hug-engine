## IDEA 1
**TITLE:** "He Barked at Every Car for 400 Days — Until the Right One Came" (62 chars | Pattern P1)
**AXES:** A3 / M3 / H3 / T1
**HUMAN:** Ray Delgado, 58. Retired Marine, 22 years. Keeps his old truck keys on a carabiner even though the truck was sold years ago — "just like the weight of 'em." Drinks coffee on the porch at 0700 sharp, military habit. His son, Marcus, 27, active-duty, deployed 14 months.
**ANIMAL:** Bruno, Labrador Retriever, 5. Rescue from a farm in Tennessee — surrendered when the farm sold. Big blocky head, a notch missing from his left ear (barbed wire, healed), tail like a whip. Barks at every single car that passes the house.
**MIRROR (M3):** Shared routine — Ray's life runs on schedule (0700 coffee, 1700 flag, 2100 lights-out); Bruno's life runs on one schedule too: every car, every time, announced. Same wound underneath: both are waiting for the one arrival that matters, and guarding against everything else.
**ARENA DETAIL:** Small ranch house outside Jacksonville, North Carolina. Long gravel driveway, porch with two rocking chairs (one always empty), flagpole by the mailbox, oak tree at the road.
**HOOK (8–18s):** Daytime. A yellow Lab stands at the edge of a gravel driveway, body rigid, barking hard at a passing sedan. Then a pickup. Then a minivan. Every car — same explosion. On the porch, a graying man sips coffee, unsurprised. A counter in the corner: DAY 400.
**MISDIRECTION:**
- *Viewer assumes:* Bruno is reactive/anxious — a rescue with car-chasing issues. The family is managing a behavior problem.
- *Real:* Bruno was a farm guardian. On the farm, his job was announcing every vehicle — friend or stranger — so the family knew. He's not anxious. He's working a job nobody gave him anymore. The behavior was misidentified for 400 days.
**TWIST POSITION:** minute 7
**CREDIBILITY ANCHOR:** C-A3 — Shelter intake record, stamped: "Owner notes: barks at vehicles. Farm dog — used to announce visitors." Dated 14 months earlier.
**KEY VISUAL:** Golden-hour. A dusty pickup turns into the gravel driveway. The Lab — mid-bark at every other car for 400 days — goes completely silent. Then launches. Dust explodes. A young Marine drops his duffel.
**SETBACK BEAT (Part 6):** Day 398: a truck identical to Marcus's old one drives past. Bruno goes silent, trembles — false alarm. Ray watches his son's dog deflate and, for the first time, says out loud: "Maybe he's just broken." Night 399: Ray takes the carabiner of old truck keys off his belt and sets them in a drawer.
**ENDING — 2 beats:**
- Setup line: "He wasn't barking at cars. He was rehearsing for one."
- Landing line: "Four hundred alarms. One arrival."

## IDEA 2
**TITLE:** "The Shelter Dog Who Chose the Wheelchair" (42 chars | Pattern P3)
**AXES:** A2 / M5 / H1 / T4
**HUMAN:** Walter Kim, 74. Navy veteran, wheelchair user (10 years). Keeps a folded newspaper in his lap even though he reads the news on a tablet — "paper feels honest." Speaks softly; laughs rarely but completely.
**ANIMAL (primary):** Biscuit, Standard Poodle, 9. Retired therapy dog — certified 6 years, 400+ facility visits. Apricot coat, shaved face, eyes that hold contact a beat too long. At the shelter 3 weeks after his handler (a nurse) moved abroad. **Second animal:** Pickles, 14-year-old orange tabby, Biscuit's kennel-mate — they sleep curled together.
**MIRROR (M5):** Role reversal — Walter spent his life being helped (the chair, the aides); Biscuit spent his life helping. Each recognizes the other's uniform: Walter sees the therapy vest folded in Biscuit's kennel; Biscuit sees the wheelchair the way he saw a hundred hospital chairs — as a workstation.
**ARENA DETAIL:** County shelter, kennel row C. Walter's accessible van in the lot. Later: Walter's bungalow — ramp, wide doorways, a sunny window seat.
**HOOK (8–18s):** Shelter kennel row. Every dog erupts as a wheelchair rolls past — except one. A poodle stands, walks calmly to the gate, and sits. Looks at the man. Looks at the chair. Wags once — the professional's greeting.
**MISDIRECTION:**
- *Viewer assumes:* Sweet story — old man adopts old therapy dog, instant bond, done.
- *Real:* Biscuit refuses to leave the kennel without the cat. He plants his feet at the shelter door and looks back. Twice. The adoption nearly falls through — until Walter says, "Then we take them both." The story was never about one animal.
**TWIST POSITION:** minute 7–8
**CREDIBILITY ANCHOR:** C-A1 — Shelter intake photo, date-stamped: Biscuit and Pickles asleep in one kennel, curled together, Biscuit's paw over the cat. Volunteer wrote on the back: "Bonded. Do not separate."
**KEY VISUAL:** Sunny window seat. Walter in his wheelchair, Biscuit's head on his lap, Pickles asleep on the wheelchair's footrest. Three generations of rescue, one sunbeam.
**SETBACK BEAT (Part 6):** Week two: Pickles hides under the bed for three days, won't eat. Walter is told "the cat may need to go back." Biscuit lies outside the bedroom door all night — the same vigil posture from his therapy days. Morning three: Pickles walks out, climbs the wheelchair footrest, and stays.
**ENDING — 2 beats:**
- Setup line: "He came for a dog. He left with a family."
- Landing line: "Some wheelchairs carry two."

## IDEA 3
**TITLE:** "She Set Two Bowls Every Night for a Year" (42 chars | Pattern P5)
**AXES:** A1 / M3 / H5 / T5
**HUMAN:** Earl Whitaker, 79. Air Force veteran, widower (wife June died 3 years ago). Sets the table for two every night out of habit — then eats standing at the counter. Winds June's old kitchen clock every Sunday though it's 10 minutes fast.
**ANIMAL:** Mabel, Basset Hound, 12. Drooping eyes, ears that sweep the floor, a waddle like a metronome. Eats slowly, one kibble at a time, like she's thinking about each one.
**MIRROR (M3):** Shared routine — Earl's evenings run on ritual (two bowls, the clock, the counter); Mabel's evenings run on ritual too (she waits by the fence at 6 PM sharp). Both are keeping appointments nobody else can see.
**ARENA DETAIL:** Small brick house in Dayton, Ohio. Kitchen with a wall clock, backyard with a wooden fence, a loose board at the back corner.
**HOOK (8–18s):** Evening kitchen. An old man sets down two dog bowls. One Basset Hound eats from the first. The second bowl sits full. The man glances at the clock: 6 PM. He looks toward the back fence.
**MISDIRECTION:**
- *Viewer assumes:* Grief ritual — the second bowl is for a dog who died, or for the deployed son's dog. The old man can't let go.
- *Real (time twist):* For a year, at 6 PM sharp, a stray slips through the loose fence board and eats from the second bowl — a scruffy terrier mix Earl has never touched, never named, never told anyone about. The "grief ritual" was a secret kindness with a schedule. When Earl's son finally visits and asks about the bowl, Earl just says, "Watch the fence at six."
**TWIST POSITION:** minute 7
**CREDIBILITY ANCHOR:** C-A4 — The neighbor, Mrs. Abernathy, 82: "I thought he was losing it, talking to an empty yard. Then I saw the little brown dog myself. Every evening. Like church."
**KEY VISUAL:** 6 PM, golden light. The loose fence board swings. A scruffy terrier mix slips through. Mabel — twelve, arthritic — lifts her head and thumps her tail once: *there you are.* Two bowls. Two dogs. One old man pretending not to watch.
**SETBACK BEAT (Part 6):** Week 40: the stray doesn't come for four nights. Earl fixes the fence board — then unfixed it. Mabel stops waiting at 6 PM. Night five: scratching at the board. Earl, 79, on his knees with a screwdriver at dusk, re-loosening the board with shaking hands.
**ENDING — 2 beats:**
- Setup line: "It was never an empty bowl. It was a reservation."
- Landing line: "Kindness keeps a schedule."

## IDEA 4
**TITLE:** "The K9 Who Flunked Retirement" (30 chars | Pattern P4)
**AXES:** A2 / M4 / H6 / T2
**HUMAN:** Sgt. Dana Cole, 38. Marine K9 handler, medically retired. Keeps her old handler's leash coiled on the hallway hook — "in case." Her daughter Lily, 9, Type 1 diabetic, wears a glucose monitor on her arm.
**ANIMAL:** Havoc, German Shepherd, 10. Retired detection K9 (explosives), 7 years service. Sable coat, gray at the temples, left ear that never fully stood up. "Flunked" retirement: won't stop working.
**MIRROR (M4):** Shared duty — Dana can't turn off the handler's vigilance (checks locks twice, scans rooms); Havoc can't turn off the nose. Mother and dog, both retired from the job, both still on shift.
**ARENA DETAIL:** Suburban home near Camp Lejeune. Lily's bedroom with a nightlight, kitchen with a candy drawer, hallway with the coiled leash.
**HOOK (8–18s):** Night. A German Shepherd paces a hallway, nose up, working. He stops at a bedroom door. Sits. Stares. Alerts — the full trained final response — on a sleeping 9-year-old girl.
**MISDIRECTION:**
- *Viewer assumes:* The old war dog is confused — alerting on the child like she's a threat, or he's stressed and "seeing" old missions. The family worries he's dangerous.
- *Real (hidden object):* Havoc isn't alerting on Lily. He's alerting on the front pocket of her backpack — where she keeps candy for blood-sugar crashes. Three nights later the glucose monitor confirms it: every alert matched a nighttime low, 20 minutes before the alarm. He retrained himself. Nobody taught him. The hidden object was the candy stash all along.
**TWIST POSITION:** minute 7–8
**CREDIBILITY ANCHOR:** C-A3 — Pediatrician's note in Lily's file: "Family reports retired detection K9 alerting 15–20 min prior to nocturnal hypoglycemic events. Correlated with CGM data on 6 occasions." Plus the vet: "The nose doesn't retire."
**KEY VISUAL:** 3 AM, blue nightlight. Havoc lying across Lily's bedroom doorway — the same blocking position from his service days — chin on paws, eyes open. On the wall: his framed service photo. Guardian, both versions.
**SETBACK BEAT (Part 6):** The family, worried, hires a trainer who says Havoc is "stressed and should be rehomed to a quiet house." Dana cries for two days. Then the endocrinologist calls: "Keep the dog. He's outperforming the monitor." The trainer's report goes in the trash; the leash stays on the hook.
**ENDING — 2 beats:**
- Setup line: "They told him to stand down. His nose outranked them."
- Landing line: "Once a guardian, always."

## IDEA 5
**TITLE:** "The Dog Who Waited at the Wrong Door" (37 chars | Pattern P2)
**AXES:** A3 / M6 / H2 / T3
**HUMAN:** Lt. Priya Nair, 33. Air Force pilot. Rents the house next door to Mrs. Abernathy, 82. Always entered through the neighbor's side gate — a shortcut across the yards she used since she moved in.
**ANIMAL:** Rocket, Australian Shepherd, 4. Rescue, one blue eye one brown. Learned the neighborhood by running it: he knows every gate, gap, and shortcut within six blocks.
**MIRROR (M6):** No mirror — Framework B, organic bond. What exists: Rocket mapped Priya's *route*, not her address. The connection is geography, not wound.
**ARENA DETAIL:** Two houses sharing a fence in San Antonio, Texas. Mrs. Abernathy's side gate (the shortcut), Priya's front door (which Rocket ignores).
**HOOK (8–18s):** Every afternoon at 5:30, an Australian Shepherd sprints past his own front door and sits at the neighbor's side gate. Staring. The owner watches from her window, baffled and slightly embarrassed.
**MISDIRECTION:**
- *Viewer assumes:* The dog likes the neighbor better — treats, attention. The owner is the second choice. Comedy with a sting.
- *Real:* For two years, Priya came home through that side gate — it was 40 seconds faster. Rocket never learned "front door." He learned *her*. When she was deployed 8 months, he kept the appointment with the gate. The dog knew the way home better than anyone. He knew first — before the family understood what he was doing.
**TWIST POSITION:** minute 6–7
**CREDIBILITY ANCHOR:** C-A4 — Mrs. Abernathy: "That dog never once waited at her own door. Not once. He sat at my gate every day at half past five like he had a train to catch. Then one day — I heard her whistle from my yard, and I understood everything."
**KEY VISUAL:** 5:30 PM, long shadows. The side gate swings open from the *other* side. A woman in flight suit steps through. The Aussie — mid-sit, eight months of waiting in his eyes — hears the whistle first. One blue eye, one brown, both suddenly wet.
**SETBACK BEAT (Part 6):** Month 6 of deployment: Mrs. Abernathy falls ill, the gate is locked for three weeks. Rocket sits at the locked gate anyway — then starts waiting at Priya's front door, confused, for the first time ever. The family fears he's "forgotten." Week 4: the gate reopens. Rocket returns to his post the same day. He never forgot. He was just locked out.
**ENDING — 2 beats:**
- Setup line: "He wasn't at the wrong door. Everyone else was."
- Landing line: "Love knows the shortcut."
